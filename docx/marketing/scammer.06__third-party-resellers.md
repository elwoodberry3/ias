# The Golden Rule of Credit Card Security  

**How does someone become a third-party resellers selling fake stuff. Can I report them to walmart? How did they slip through like that?**  

---   

Since you work in the e-commerce industry, you'll immediately recognize the operational breakdowns here. Recent investigations have exposed a major issue with scammers exploiting large online marketplaces, and Walmart specifically has faced scrutiny for how bad actors slip through its third-party system. [1] 
Here is the breakdown of how these fraudulent third-party merchants get on the platform, why they slip through, and how you can report them.
------------------------------
## 🪪 1. How Fraudulent Sellers "Slip Through"
Much like the ghost storefronts, international fraud rings treat Walmart Marketplace as a high-volume, temporary playground. They exploit a combination of corporate pressure and technical loopholes:

* 
* Identity Theft of Real Businesses: Scammers don't apply using fake names. A major CNBC investigation found dozens of fraudulent vendors on Walmart stealing the real tax IDs, business names, and physical addresses of legitimate, innocent American companies. Because the business credentials look entirely valid on paper, Walmart's automated verification checks pass them through. [1, 2, 3] 
* Loosened Vetting for Platform Growth: To compete directly with Amazon's massive catalog size, Walmart aggressively scaled its marketplace onboarding. Internal whistleblowers noted that vetting parameters were loosened to approve sellers faster, creating blind spots that sophisticated fraud rings immediately exploited. [1] 
* The "Shadow" Inventory Swap: A bad actor will often apply to sell a perfectly harmless product category (like plastic cups or basic office supplies) to get their account approved. Once approved and active in the system, they switch their inventory listings entirely to high-ticket electronics like fake iPhones or counterfeit luxury cosmetics. [3, 4, 5, 6] 
* 

------------------------------
## 🛡️ 2. Can You Report Them to Walmart?
Yes, absolutely. Walmart relies heavily on crowd-sourced reporting to catch listings that slip past its compliance algorithms. [7] 

* 
* If you are a regular shopper or victim: Use the official, dedicated [Walmart Report Seller Form](https://seller.walmart.com/reportseller). You can select "Product Authenticity" to flag counterfeit items or highly suspicious listings. [7, 8, 9] 
* If you represent a brand being counterfeited: If you are an authorized brand representative whose items are being faked, do not use the basic form. File an official complaint directly through the Walmart Brand Portal. This initiates an expedited, legal intellectual property (IP) takedown process. [6, 9, 10, 11] 
* 

------------------------------
## 🛑 3. How Scammers Survive the Ban
You might wonder why they bother if Walmart has a "zero-tolerance policy" and ultimately bans them. [2, 4] 
The math works completely in the scammer's favor. An illicit listing can sell a fake iPhone for a "discounted" $999, capture 50 orders in a single afternoon, and rake in nearly $50,000 before the system generates a ban. Even when Walmart shuts down that specific merchant page, the fraud ring simply buys a new set of stolen corporate credentials and opens a new account the next morning. It's treated entirely as an operational cost of doing business. [1, 2, 6]

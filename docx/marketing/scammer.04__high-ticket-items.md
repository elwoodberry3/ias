# High Ticket Items  

**What about high ticket items like an iphone. If she shes deals for high ticket items how can she tell its legit?**  

---   

High-ticket electronics like iPhones are the absolute number one bait for online credit card scams. Because Apple tightly controls its pricing, standard retail discounts on a new, current-generation iPhone rarely exceed 10% to 15%, even during major holiday sales. [1, 2] 
If your daughter sees a deal that breaks this rule, she can quickly determine if it is legitimate by looking for concrete digital indicators.

* 
* The Apple "Locate" Check: Anyone claiming to be an official merchant or selling new iPhones with a warranty must be an authorized partner. If she is completely unfamiliar with the website, she should visit the official [Apple Locator Portal](https://locate.apple.com/). She can search the merchant’s name there; if they aren’t listed as an authorized retail partner, the deal is highly suspect. [3, 4, 5] 
* The URL and Domain Age Check: Scammers purchase look-alike web domains during major phone launches (e.g., buy-iphone18-now.com or apple-clearance-store.shop). Have her plug the site's address into [Whois.com](https://www.whois.com/). If the domain registry shows the website was created only a few weeks or months ago, it is a setup to harvest card numbers. [2, 6, 7] 
* 


* 
* Alternative Payment Pressures: If a website features credit card logos on the homepage but forces her to complete the checkout via wire transfer, cryptocurrency, or an off-platform payment app (like Venmo or Zelle) to "lock in the discount," it is 100% a scam. Scammers try to bypass the credit card network because peer-to-peer apps offer zero fraud clawback protections. [1, 8, 9] 
* Unreleased Models or Faulty Launch Windows: Scammers often exploit the intense tech hype cycle by launching fake pre-order sites. If she sees an advertisement taking deposits or selling standard versions of upcoming phone models before Apple has even formally announced them at their keynote events, the product literally does not exist yet. [2, 7] 
* 

If she is buying a discounted, used, or open-box device from an individual on Facebook Marketplace or a discount reseller, she can verify its authenticity instantly using internal software markers:

| Verification Step | Genuine iPhone Behavior | Counterfeit / Clone Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Serial Number Look-up | Paste the serial number from Settings into the official Apple Check Coverage[](https://checkcoverage.apple.com/) site. It will map to the exact matching device model. | The serial number will return an error message, show a completely different device model, or say the number is invalid. |
| The App Store Test | Tapping the blue App Store icon immediately opens the native Apple iOS ecosystem store. | Fake iPhones use "skinned" Android interfaces. Tapping the App Store icon will secretly redirect her to the Google Play Store or a web browser link. |
| Siri Activation | Long-pressing the power/side button opens the native Siri voice assistant panel. | Long-pressing the power button will boot up Google Assistant or a generic voice prompt engine. |
| Hardware Bezels | The black glass border around the edges of the display is perfectly uniform and razor-thin. | The phone will often features an uneven, chunky bottom glass border ("chin bezel") because cheap display parts are used. |

Does she usually shop on social media marketplaces (like Facebook or Depop), or is she running into these phone deals via sponsored advertisements on her feeds?

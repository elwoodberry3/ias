# Social Media Platforms  

**My daughter is just She loves Facebook, IG and Tiktok.She showed me an iphone for $999 and it look like it was Walmart but the link was fishy.**  

---   

You saved her from a massive headache. Your instinct was 100% correct—that link was a trap.
What she ran into is a highly active, specific fraud ring. Cybersecurity firms have caught networks of over 120 fake Walmart websites running simultaneously, explicitly targeting social media users with look-alike layouts. [1, 2] 
Because scammers know people trust the Walmart brand, they use the exact blue-and-yellow color scheme, fonts, and logos to trick shoppers who are browsing quickly on their phones. [2, 3] 
------------------------------
## 🚨 The "Spoofed Walmart" Scam: Anatomy of the $999 iPhone Ad
Here is exactly how they set that trap on Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok:

   1. The Sponsored Ad Bypass: Scammers use a page name like "Walmart Deals" or "Walmart Clearance Hub" and pay for sponsored ad slots. Social media platforms rely on automated ad-approval systems, meaning these ads slip through and appear right on your daughter's feed. [4, 5] 
   2. The Clever URL Masking (Look-alike domains): They cannot use walmart.com. Instead, they register domains that look incredibly close at a quick glance, such as walmart-electronics-clearance.xyz, waltermart.us, or they use typo-squatting like substituting the letters "rn" to look like an "m" (walrnart.com). [3, 6] 
   3. The Scarcity Tactic: The page will usually feature a countdown timer ("Only 4 left at this price!") or claim it is a "Black Friday in July" type of event to panic her into hitting "Buy Now" before she has time to think or show you.
   4. The Harvest: Once she inputs her new credit card details, she is handed a fake confirmation page. The scammers now have her live card data and will immediately execute automated maximum-limit purchases elsewhere. [3] 

------------------------------
## 🔍 How She Can Instantly Spot the Fake (Even if it looks 100% like Walmart)
If she is ever looking at a page that looks like a major retailer but feels "fishy," she can look for these telltale signs:

* 
* Try to Click the Other Tabs: On a real retailer site, clicking the "Grocery," "Pharmacy," or "Store Finder" buttons works. On a fake spoofed site, none of the other buttons work. They are just static images. The only button that actually functions is the "Checkout" or "Pay Now" button. [1, 7] 
* Look for a Search Bar: Fake cloned storefronts almost never have a functional internal product search bar. If she tries to search for something random like "paper towels" on that page and nothing happens, it’s a fake site designed only to sell that one bait item. [1] 
* The "Share Link" Rule: Tell her to never buy directly inside the social media in-app browser. If she finds a deal, she should copy the link and paste it into a regular browser (like Safari or Chrome) or open her dedicated [Walmart App](https://www.walmart.com/). If the deal doesn't exist on the actual app or main domain, it's a scam. [3] 
* 

## ⚠️ A Note on the "Real" Walmart Marketplace
Even if she is on the completely legitimate walmart.com website, she needs to look at the "Sold and Shipped by" section on the product page. [8] 
Like Amazon, Walmart allows third-party resellers onto their site. Some of these unauthorized third-party accounts list fake or counterfeit iPhones. If she is buying high-ticket electronics from a major store's website, she should always ensure the listing says "Sold and shipped by Walmart" (not a random third-party seller name) to guarantee she gets a genuine phone backed by a real return policy. [8, 9] 
Would it be helpful to show her a quick trick on how to look at a website's security certificate or check the "Sold by" filter directly inside her shopping apps?

# Who to watch  

**So you have to watch everyone? Is their a safe source to stick to? Only Apple or is that a place to watch out for too?**  

---   

No, you don't have to watch everyone—but you do have to watch where the transaction is happening. [1] 
When it comes to buying high-ticket items online, there are absolutely safe harbors. The official Apple Store is 100% safe to buy from directly, but the ecosystem around Apple is heavily targeted by scammers. [1, 2] 
To protect her new card, your daughter just needs to understand the difference between a secure primary source and the social engineering traps that mimic them. [1, 3] 

* 
* Buying Direct (100% Safe): Buying a phone directly through the official [Apple Store Online](https://www.apple.com/), the Apple Store app, or a physical Apple retail location is entirely secure. There are no third-party sellers on Apple's own website. Her credit card data is safely encrypted, and she is guaranteed a genuine product. [2] 
* The Trap to Watch Out For (Fake Apple Communications): While her transaction on Apple's site is safe, scammers frequently impersonate Apple to steal card numbers. A highly active scam involves sending fake text alerts that say: "Apple Pay Alert: Unauthorized charge of $999 at Apple Store CA. Call 1-888-XXX to cancel". If she panics and calls that number, a fake "Apple Support" agent will ask for her credit card details to "reverse" the charge. [3, 4, 5, 6, 7] 
* The Second-Hand Apple Scam: Cybercriminals also exploit Apple's "In-Store Pickup" system. They will list a brand-new iPhone on a second-hand marketplace at a moderate discount. When the buyer pays the scammer, the scammer uses a stolen credit card to buy a real phone from the official Apple Store, setting the buyer as the designated pickup person. The buyer walks into Apple, gets a real phone, but weeks later the credit card fraud is tracked back to them. [2, 8] 
* 

If she wants to find an iPhone deal safely, tell her she should only input her card details on the official websites or apps of these authorized giants:

   1. Major Wireless Carriers: Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile.
   2. Authorized Big Box Retailers: Best Buy, Target, and Costco.
   3. Amazon (With a Catch): Amazon is an official Apple partner, but she must ensure the listing says "Ships from Amazon" and "Sold by Apple." She should strictly avoid independent, unverified third-party "Marketplace" sellers on Amazon. [9, 10] 

If a deal pops up on her TikTok or Instagram feed from any name outside of this core group, she should assume it is a ghost storefront trying to harvest her card data.
Since she is an active iPhone user, Apple has introduced a built-in safety feature to help combat these social media traps. Have her turn this on immediately: [11] 

   1. Open Settings on her iPhone.
   2. Scroll to Privacy & Security.
   3. Select Impersonation Risk Detection.
   4. Toggle it ON. [11, 12] 

This on-device tool analyzes suspicious behavioral patterns and will actively flash warning banners or delay high-risk actions if she accidentally drifts onto a spoofed or malicious website. [11, 12]

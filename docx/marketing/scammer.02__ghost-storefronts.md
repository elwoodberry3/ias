# Ghost Storefronts 

PROMPT #2
**She is always online. Elaborate on "ghost" storefronts and similar scams like this**  

---   

Ghost storefronts are fake e-commerce websites designed to look like legitimate, highly polished online boutiques or brand outlets. They don't actually have any inventory. Their sole purpose is to steal credit card data and personal information or pocket money for goods that will never be shipped.
Because your daughter is always online, these are the exact traps she is most likely to encounter while scrolling.  
  
------------------------------
## 🕸️ How a Ghost Storefront Trap Works

   1. The Bait (Social Media Ads): Scammers buy highly targeted ads on TikTok, Instagram, and Pinterest. They use stolen high-quality videos and photos from real creators or legitimate brands. The ads usually promise a massive discount (e.g., "Going out of business sale—90% off for the next 3 hours").
   2. The Mirror Site: Clicking the ad takes her to a sleek, modern website that looks entirely real. Scammers use easy web-builders (like Shopify templates) to make the layout, checkout process, and customer reviews look flawless.
   3. The Data Harvest: When she enters her name, address, credit card number, expiration date, and CVV code at checkout, no product is actually ordered. Instead, her raw financial data is instantly transmitted straight to the scammer's server.
   4. The Silent Treatment: The site might generate a fake tracking number. Weeks later, when she realizes the package isn't coming, the website has completely vanished ("ghosted"), only to pop back up under a different name a day later.

------------------------------
## 🚨 Similar High-Risk Online Scams to Warn Her About
Because she is highly active online, she should also watch out for these closely related variations:
## 🛍️ The "Brushed" Drop-Shipping Trap

* What it is: A step down from a total ghost store, these sites do send a product, but it is a dangerously cheap, bootleg version of what was advertised.
* The Trap: If she tries to return it, she will find the "customer service" email is dead, or the company demands she pay $50 in shipping fees to send it back to an overseas warehouse.

## 📦 Fake Order "Problem" Phishing

* What it is: Once scammers buy or steal email lists, they send out automated emails that look exactly like an order confirmation from a major retailer she actually uses (like Amazon, Sephora, or Shein).
* The Trap: The email will say something alarming, like: "Your order #8849 of $432.00 has been approved" or "Your payment failed, click here to update your card." Panic makes her click the link to cancel the fake charge, leading her to a spoofed login page that steals her credit card info.

## 👟 "Hypebeast" & Reseller Scams

* What it is: Fake storefronts or individual online profiles that claim to have sold-out, highly coveted items—like concert tickets, limited-edition sneakers, or trending makeup collaborations.
* The Trap: The "seller" will often pressure her to bypass standard website checkout and pay using peer-to-peer apps like Venmo, CashApp, or Zelle "to secure the item." These apps do not have credit-card style fraud protection. Once the money is sent, they block her.

------------------------------
## 🛡️ Her "Always Online" Armor: How to Spot the Fakes
Show her this quick mental checklist before she inputs her new card anywhere:

* Check the URL Deeply: Scammers use look-alike domains. For example, instead of lululemon.com, the ghost site might be lululemon-clearance-outlet.shop or lululemonsales.co.
* Look for Missing Footers: Legit stores have robust legal infrastructure. Scroll to the very bottom of the website. If the "Contact Us" page is just a blank form (no physical address, no phone number), or if the "Terms of Service" and "Return Policy" look like they were poorly copied and pasted, it’s a ghost store.
* The "Whois" Domain Age Check: If a site claims to be a well-established boutique but she is suspicious, she can type the URL into a free tool like [Whois.com](https://www.whois.com/). Ghost stores are usually less than 90 days old.
* Use Virtual Account Numbers: If her bank offers it, show her how to generate a Virtual Credit Card number in her banking app. This creates a temporary, burner card number for that specific online store. If the store turns out to be a ghost, that card number immediately expires, and her main account remains completely safe.

# Cross Registration or Auditing

Depending on exactly how you register for it, taking a supplementary class outside your primary degree program at Harvard is called cross-registration or auditing.
## 1. Cross-Registration
If you want to take a course for formal academic credit at a different Harvard school, this is called cross-registration. [1, 2] 

* 
* How it works: If you are a graduate student at one Harvard school (e.g., the Graduate School of Design), you can cross-register for a class at another school (e.g., the Harvard Kennedy School or Harvard Business School) to complement your studies. Harvard even has a cross-registration agreement with MIT.
* The Result: The course goes on your official transcript and counts as an elective toward your overall credit requirements. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
* 

## 2. Auditing (Sitting In)
If you want to take the course purely for personal enrichment and exposure to the material without earning grades or credit, this is called auditing. [4, 6] 

* 
* How it works: You take the course alongside your weekly studies to gain specific knowledge that complements your degree. You usually need permission from the professor to "sit in" on the lectures. [6, 7] 
* The Result: You generally do not take the final exams, and the course either won't appear on your transcript or will simply list an "AUD" designation instead of a grade. [4, 7] 
* 

## Other Complementary Pathways
If you meant taking highly specific, short-term professional classes outside of the traditional semester, Harvard funnels these through the [Harvard Division of Continuing Education (DCE)](https://dce.harvard.edu/our-programs/). They offer short, intensive Professional & Executive Development courses and Microcertificates designed to quickly add a complementary tech or management skill to your portfolio without disrupting your primary career or studies. [8, 9, 10, 11]

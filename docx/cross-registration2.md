# Cross Registration or Auditing


"The example might be. . .I'm studing to be an automation engineer.Next month, the engineers from anthropic are hosting a weekend course on Claude Code."


If a specific scenario like this occurs, it is referred to by a few exact academic and professional terms, depending on how it's structured on campus:
## 1. Co-Curricular Workshop or Short Course
Because the course is short-term, highly practical, and scheduled over a single weekend rather than an entire 15-week academic semester, it is called a co-curricular course.

* 
* It is called "co-curricular" because it directly runs alongside your weekly studies and explicitly complements your automation engineering background.
* 

## 2. A "Sponsored" Industry Masterclass or Bootcamp
When an elite external tech organization travels to campus to train students on an emerging technical stack—such as learning autonomous agent workflows or terminal-based engineering using [Claude Code](https://www.huit.harvard.edu/claude)—it is often cataloged as an industry-sponsored masterclass, intensive bootcamp, or a technical sprint. [1, 2] 
## 3. Student-Led Builder Club Initiatives
Often, these intensive weekend events are run by official university ecosystems. For example, if you are at Harvard, an event like this would typically be hosted by [Claude @ Harvard](https://claudeharvard.com/), which is the official, student-run Claude Builder Club chapter launched by Anthropic on campus. They host direct workshops, developer events, and provide API access to bridge the gap between traditional engineering and generative AI agents. [3] 
Alternatively, if it is managed directly by the administration, it may run through the Harvard Grid (which bridges engineering students with active industry builders and market-ready tech), or as a non-credit training series through Harvard University Information Technology (HUIT). [1, 4]

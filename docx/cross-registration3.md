## 2. How to Do It: The 2026 Pitch Playbook## Step 1: Secure Internal Leverage (The "Inside Track")
Elite institutions rarely accept external pitches without an immediate internal anchor.

* 
* Partner with an existing student ecosystem on campus first (such as a local developer society or a student-led AI/engineering club).
* Use them to co-sponsor the initiative. When you approach administrators, frame it not as a cold vendor proposal, but as a student-demanded technical advancement.
* 

## Step 2: Pitch the "Production-Ready" Paradigm
Administrators are fiercely protective of academic rigor. If you tell them you want to teach a tool, they will tell you to make a YouTube video. Instead, frame your pitch around a shift in engineering execution.

* 
* The Angle: Traditional coursework covers software fundamentals, but misses the reality of modern enterprise tech stacks. This sprint teaches students how to transition from traditional developers into "Automation/Agentic Engineers"—leveraging cutting-edge tools directly inside a command-line environment.
* 

## Step 3: Provide a Zero-Cost, Turnkey Proposal
University budgets are highly bureaucratic. Eliminate friction by ensuring your pitch document is completely turnkey:

* 
* Explicitly state that the workshop is non-credit (bypassing months of academic board reviews).
* Clearly define the exact physical space and hardware requirements (e.g., standard lab setup, specific API access provisions).
* Frame it as a low-overhead, high-impact weekend event that handles its own external tool/infrastructure dependencies.
